## 1. Universal rules (every format)

**Canvas:** 1080×1920 vertical, 30 fps (60 fps for some home shoots). No color grade, natural light.

**Language:** Spanish first, with natural English drops — *sleep training, sleep regression, by the way, tummy time, play mat, sleep deprivation, got this bro*. Keep the English words exactly as he says them in captions; don't translate.

**Pacing:**
- Speech is tight: **~205–230 words per minute** once edited. Dead air is removed; remaining pauses ≤ ~0.5 s.
- **First cut lands at 0.8–2.5 s.** The hook never sits longer than 2.5 s.
- Talking segments ~2–3.5 s; montage/B-roll shots ~0.5–1 s.

**Hooks (spoken):**
- Signature opener: **"Nadie me dijo esto…"** (v2, v3) followed immediately by the topic.
- Direct address: **"Vas a ser papá por primera vez, felicidades — tres cosas que…"** (v4)
- Third-person intro: **"Hola amigos del Daily Dad, soy su esposa…"** (v1)
- Always state the promise early: "aquí están los cinco…", "tres cosas que van a cambiar…".

**Hook visual (home series):** he leans into the lens, very close and low, then cuts to the main shot.

**Structure:** numbered points ("uno / dos / tres", "número cinco", "y el bonus").

**Endings:** a warm sign-off line, not a hard sell — *"Aprendan de nuestra experiencia"*, *"Haz lo que te funcione a ti y a tu familia"*, *"Got this bro"*. Optionally a playful exit or gag.

**Audio:** the references are quiet (−18 to −29 LUFS integrated). Instagram plays well at **about −14 LUFS**, so I'll normalise louder than the originals unless you say otherwise. A soft music bed shows up under the car format.

## 4. Caption systems (pick one per format)

1. **One word at a time** (home talk, v3): single white bold word above and to the right of his head, in sync with speech.
2. **Phrase captions, lower centre** (home list v2, car v4, Q&A v5): 1–2 lines, white, about 3–8 words per screen, no background box. Phrase breaks follow his breath groups.
3. **Yellow emphasis** (car v4): number words (*uno, dos*) and key words (*triple*) show alone in neon yellow `#ECFF0E`.

## 5. Formats

### A. Home list — "Cosas importantes de bebés" (v2)
- 0–2.5 s: close lean-in hook plus the series title card (blue pill, yellow age pill, cursor click, stickers).
- Desk shot, holding a lav mic. A two-colour topic title stays on top the whole video, with a numbered sub-point line under it that changes per item.
- Hold up each product as he names it. A product cut-out sticker or B-roll inset sits beside him.
- Phrase captions at lower centre.
- About 35 cuts/min: a jump cut on almost every phrase.
- End: "y el bonus…", then a sign-off line and a quick product close-up.

### B. Home talk — long explainer, "Part N" (v3)
- Same title card with a big yellow **"PART 9"**.
- Locked-off seated shot, mic in hand, topic title fixed at the top ("SLEEP" cream + "TRAINING" blue).
- **One-word captions** top right.
- Variety comes from **handheld selfie close-up inserts** (lean-in, smiling) every 15–40 s, and graphic overlays such as the Ferber chart.
- Longest format (about 3 min); cuts are fewer but jump cuts still remove all dead air.

### C. Car POV (v4)
- Handwritten "POV / ESTÁS A PUNTO DE SER PAPÁ" title fixed at the top for the whole video.
- Single handheld front-camera shot; jump cuts about every 2–3 s.
- Phrase captions at lower centre, with neon-yellow single words for numbers and emphasis.
- Continuous soft music bed; no silences left in.
- Ends on encouragement: "lo vas a lograr… got this bro".

### D. Day-in-the-life (v1)
- Opening title: "Stay at home dad / Papá de 2 / **7am-10am**" (time range in neon yellow).
- **Voiceover by your wife**, playful and teasing ("síganlo porque ya yo no quiero trabajar").
- Rapid montage: **~89 cuts/min, shots ~0.8 s**, lots of POV and handheld angles (doors, locks, coffee, kids).
- A clock timestamp in the centre of every shot (7:03 → 9:07) drives the story.
- No spoken-word captions; the voiceover carries it.

### E. Family Q&A game (v5)
- Group seated in a row, one locked-off wide shot plus close-up reaction punch-ins.
- Coloured handwritten name labels over each person at the start.
- The question shows at the top in outlined bubbly handwriting and stays until the next question.
- Answers appear as small white phrase captions at lower centre; laughter beats are left in (the only format with real pauses).

### F. Split-screen vlog — "Date Night" (v6, newest edit, a direction you want more of)
The most produced edit of the six, and the shortest. What sets it apart:
- **Dual-perspective shooting:** you and your wife film each other at the same time, phones facing each other. Both reactions to the same moment (cheers, first bite, stealing dessert) play **stacked on screen together**.
- **Multi-panel layouts on black:** the frame splits into 2–3 horizontal strips (sometimes a staggered collage) with thin black gaps. The layout changes about every 0.7 s, and panels slide in, so parts of the frame are briefly black.
- **Mixed coverage in the panels:** selfies, POV walking shots, motion-blur whips, food close-ups and venue details (logo, Halloween decor).
- **Title:** "DATE / NIGHT" in big neon yellow (same family as `#ECFF0E`), split over the seam between two panels and laid over the venue logo.
- **Captions:** **one small white bold word at a time**, sitting on the seam between panels.
- **Pacing:** 21 s total, about 45 cuts/min, median shot 0.7 s, **no pauses**. Lines overlap the music, and the audio is louder (−16 LUFS).
- **Script shape:** recommendation hook ("Si estás buscando un plan para este fin de semana…"), venue name, what you ate (Halloween black pizza), a playful bit ("me voy a comer tu postre / ey, no, ese es mi postre"), then a cute sign-off.
- **Shooting tip:** this only works if both phones record at once. Start both cameras together, and clap or clink glasses at the start so I can sync the two angles automatically from the audio.
- **Who builds it:** CapCut's split/collage layouts can do it by hand. I can also assemble the panels, sync and word captions here with FFmpeg/Hyperframes, which saves the most manual work of any format.

## 6. How I'll use this when editing your raw footage

1. Transcribe with whisper.cpp (multilingual `small` model), keeping the English words.
2. Pick the format (A–E), find the hook moment, and cut dead air and false starts to hit ~210 wpm.
3. Build the rough cut with FFmpeg at source resolution, with no grade and short audio fades at each cut.
4. Generate captions in the right system (SRT for CapCut, or burned-in with Hyperframes/Remotion if you want it finished here).
5. Hand over the rough cut, an edit table, a CapCut finishing plan and a list of retakes to check.

Transcripts are in `transcripts/`; whisper's spelling is roughly 90% right on names (e.g. "fervor method" = Ferber method).
